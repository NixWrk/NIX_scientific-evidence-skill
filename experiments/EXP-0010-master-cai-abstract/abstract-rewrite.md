## Revised Abstract

**Background — problem.** After hospital discharge, patients with heart failure
may have difficulty recognizing and responding to worsening symptoms, making
feasible home self-monitoring an important practical question
[SRC-AAR-002-B0010].

**Objective — study question.** This pilot study assessed the feasibility of a
symptom diary and daily noninvasive lung-impedance measurements at home for 30
days after discharge and described changes in symptoms, impedance, and self-care
behavior [SRC-AAR-002-B0011].

**Methods — design and participants.** A prospective longitudinal study enrolled
10 patients with severe heart failure (NYHA class III or IV) from two university
hospitals in Norway and Lithuania. Participants recorded symptoms with the
Memorial Symptom Assessment Scale for Heart Failure and measured lung impedance
with the Edema Guard Monitor; informal caregivers assisted with the measurements
[SRC-AAR-002-B0012].

**Results — feasibility and measured change.** Seven participants provided data
for the full 30-day period. The study received 262 of 300 planned impedance
measurements (87%) and 1332 of 1500 planned diary entries (89%)
[SRC-AAR-002-B0080]. The median standardized self-care score increased from 56
(IQR 22–75) at discharge to 81 (IQR 72–98) at 30 days (`P=.007`)
[SRC-AAR-002-B0097]. Measurements were obtained with caregiver assistance and
daily access to a heart-failure study nurse, conditions that are not routine in
the two participating countries [SRC-AAR-002-B0080; SRC-AAR-002-B0102].

**Conclusion — bounded interpretation.** The findings support 30-day process
feasibility for diary and lung-impedance self-monitoring under the support
conditions studied. Because the sample comprised 10 participants, the daily
symptom instrument was unvalidated, and variability precluded analysis of
symptom–impedance associations, the study does not establish predictive utility
or reduced readmissions [SRC-AAR-002-B0108; SRC-AAR-002-B0110].
