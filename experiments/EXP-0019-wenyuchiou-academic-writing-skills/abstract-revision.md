## Revised text

**Background.** After hospital discharge, patients with heart failure may have
difficulty recognizing and responding to worsening symptoms. This problem
motivates evaluation of symptom and fluid-status monitoring at home
[SRC-AAR-002-B0010].

**Objective.** This pilot study assessed the feasibility of a symptom diary and
daily noninvasive lung-impedance measurements at home for 30 days after
discharge. It also described changes in symptoms, impedance, and self-care
behavior during follow-up [SRC-AAR-002-B0011].

**Methods.** The prospective longitudinal study enrolled 10 patients with NYHA
class III or IV heart failure from two university hospitals in Norway and
Lithuania. Participants recorded symptoms and measured lung impedance with
assistance from informal caregivers [SRC-AAR-002-B0012].

**Results.** Seven participants provided data for the complete 30-day period.
Participants submitted 262 of 300 planned lung-impedance measurements (87%) and
1332 of 1500 planned symptom-diary entries (89%) [SRC-AAR-002-B0080]. The median
standardized self-care score increased from 56 (IQR 22–75) at discharge to 81
(IQR 72–98) after 30 days (`P=.007`) [SRC-AAR-002-B0097]. Measurements were
performed with caregiver assistance, and participants had daily access to a
heart-failure study nurse [SRC-AAR-002-B0080; SRC-AAR-002-B0102].

**Conclusions.** The completion rates support the feasibility of 30-day home
monitoring in this small pilot under the assistance conditions studied. The
observed self-care change does not establish that monitoring caused the change,
predicted deterioration, or reduced readmissions. The sample of 10, variability
that precluded symptom–impedance association analysis, and use of an unvalidated
daily symptom measure limit broader inference [SRC-AAR-002-B0108;
SRC-AAR-002-B0110].
