## Revised text

**Background.** After hospital discharge, patients with heart failure may have
difficulty recognizing and responding to worsening symptoms. This challenge
motivates evaluation of home-based symptom and fluid-status monitoring
[SRC-AAR-002-B0010].

**Objective.** This pilot study assessed whether patients could use a symptom
diary and perform daily noninvasive lung-impedance measurements at home for 30
days after discharge. It also described changes in symptoms, impedance, and
self-care behavior during follow-up [SRC-AAR-002-B0011].

**Methods.** The prospective longitudinal study enrolled 10 patients with NYHA
class III or IV heart failure from two university hospitals in Norway and
Lithuania. Participants recorded symptoms with the Memorial Symptom Assessment
Scale for Heart Failure and used the Edema Guard Monitor with assistance from
informal caregivers [SRC-AAR-002-B0012].

**Results.** Seven participants provided data for the complete 30-day period.
Participants submitted 262 of 300 planned lung-impedance measurements (87%) and
1332 of 1500 planned symptom-diary entries (89%) [SRC-AAR-002-B0080]. The median
standardized self-care score increased from 56 (IQR 22–75) at discharge to 81
(IQR 72–98) after 30 days (`P=.007`) [SRC-AAR-002-B0097]. Measurements were made
with caregiver assistance, and all participants had daily access to a
heart-failure study nurse [SRC-AAR-002-B0080; SRC-AAR-002-B0102].

**Conclusions.** The observed completion rates indicate that daily symptom-diary
and lung-impedance monitoring was practicable for this 10-participant pilot under
the support conditions studied. The increase in self-care score describes change
during follow-up but does not establish that monitoring reduced readmissions or
predicted deterioration. The small sample, variability that precluded analysis of
symptom–impedance associations, and use of an unvalidated daily symptom measure
limit broader inference [SRC-AAR-002-B0108; SRC-AAR-002-B0110].
